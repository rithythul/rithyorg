Walking the newly developed streets of Siem Reap, you notice old and new side by side. The new sidewalks were built around the ancient trees instead of cutting them down. Like the roots of those trees running under the pavement, Siem Reap's potential as a tech hub runs through both its history and its present.

The city has recently attracted a noticeable number of young couples. They come for quality of life and for opportunity, and in doing so they follow the scholars who once came to this region to learn.

Few people realize that Ta Prohm, now famous for its tree-covered ruins, was once the largest university of its time, a center of learning that drew students and scholars from across the region. That history is a reminder that Siem Reap has been a place of knowledge before, and it could be again through technology.

Phnom Penh has established infrastructure, and Sihanoukville has strategic trade advantages. Siem Reap offers something different: a place where new work can grow alongside heritage. Its new developments, with wide sidewalks and preserved trees, are what tech workers and digital nomads increasingly look for: quality of life together with opportunity.

Perhaps Siem Reap is already choosing for us, as it did centuries ago when it became a center of learning. The young professionals moving in, the careful urban development, and its history as a place of learning all point to it as Cambodia's future tech city.

The answer to where Cambodia's tech city should be may not come from the obvious measures of existing infrastructure or economic zones. It may come from what makes a place good to live in and good to think in. Siem Reap, with its history, its growing community of young professionals, and its careful development, makes a strong case.

Watching young couples choose to build their futures here, we may be seeing the early stage of a renewal in a place that has been a center of learning before. Sometimes a promising future grows from a fertile past, and Siem Reap's soil has held knowledge for centuries.

In the end, choosing a tech city is not only about infrastructure or economics. It is about a place where new ideas can grow naturally, where quality of life feeds creativity, and where the past can inform the future. Seen that way, Siem Reap is a serious candidate.
