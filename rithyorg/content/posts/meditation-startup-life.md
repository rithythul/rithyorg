I meditate for short periods between working hours, and I meditate on bad news before I respond. It is not a ritual for show. It is the most practical tool I have found in thirteen years of building startups.

A startup makes everything feel urgent. Answer the investor now. Fix the server now. Hire someone yesterday. Live inside that urgency and you stop thinking; you only react.

Meditation trains the pause between what happens and what you do. In vipassana you watch a thought or a sensation arrive, and you do not act on it. The same skill works at a desk: see the problem whole, then respond.

Stand as a sentry over your own mind and watch each impression as it arrives. Criticism of the product is not an attack on you, but ego cannot tell the difference. Pride defends a bad idea long after logic has let it go.

Vipassana teaches impermanence. The server crash that feels like ruin today is forgotten next month. Remember that, and you stop overreacting to problems that pass.

It changes how I meet people too. With investors, I listen for what they want to know instead of trying to impress them, and the anxiety that makes founders oversell goes quiet. When someone brings me a problem, I wait before offering an answer. Often they need to talk it through more than they need my solution.

Meditation does not make you passive. It makes you responsive instead of reactive. The busier the week, the more the pause is worth.

The practice is simple. Sit. Watch the breath. When the mind runs to the next business problem, notice it and come back. Before a hire or a hard decision, sit first. The decision will wait, and you will meet it with a clearer mind.

**The world moves fast enough. Do not let your mind make it faster.**
