# Manual of Style (MoS) for Nimmit's Writing

## Core Principles

1. **No em‑dashes** – use commas, semicolons, or rephrase instead.
   - Example: “He was tired—but he kept going.” → “He was tired, but he kept going.”

2. **Replace all acronyms and jargon** with concrete alternatives that preserve meaning.
   - Spell out acronyms at first use, then consider replacing with plain language.
   - Example: “FOIA request” → “request for government records under the Freedom of Information Act.”
   - If the acronym must remain, explain its function in concrete terms (what it does, what it affects).

3. **Make every unexplained term obvious** through context, action, or tangible detail.
   - Do not assume prior knowledge; show what the term means via a scene or example.
   - Example: Instead of “equal opportunity files,” show a folder labeled “Hiring Decisions – 2024” with papers inside.

4. **Use experiential writing** – show via sensory details, actions, and images; avoid pure exposition.
   - Let the reader see, hear, feel what is happening.
   - Example: “The server room hummed with cooling fans” instead of “The server room was noisy.”

5. **Every sentence must stand clear on its own** – no reliance on prior knowledge not supplied in the text.
   - Each sentence should be understandable without needing earlier sentences.
   - Use analogies or concrete images to explain complex ideas.

6. **Paragraphs must end on a concrete image, action, or sensory detail** – not on an abstraction or summary statement.
   - End with something the reader can picture: a blinking light, a closing door, a hand on a keyboard.
   - Avoid ending with “because,” “since,” “as,” or “due to” (see Rule 7).

7. **Ban causal‑sentence endings** – do not end a sentence with “because,” “since,” “as,” or “due to.”
   - Causality should be shown through structure, not tacked on at the end.

8. **Sentence length** – aim for an average of 7‑9 words; no sentence may exceed 20 words.
   - Vary length for rhythm, but keep most sentences short.

9. **Adverb usage** – keep ‑ly adverbs below 0.2% of total words; prefer strong verbs.
   - Replace “quickly ran” with “sprinted.”

10. **Voice** – favor active voice; limit passive constructions to less than 10% of clauses.
    - Example: “The ball was thrown by John” → “John threw the ball.”

11. **Clarity pass** – for each chapter, verify that any term or concept not self‑evident from the surrounding sentences is explained via a concrete image, a specific action, or a simple analogy.
    - After writing, scan for jargon and ensure each is made tangible.

12. **Show essentials** – if a concept is critical, illustrate it through an example or image rather than a definition.
    - Do not define “database”; show a row of blinking lights representing data.
    - Show the human impact: a denied claim, a delayed payment, a family waiting.

13. **Inline definitions must be complete standalone sentences** – not appended fragments.
    - When defining a term within the text, write it as a full sentence.
    - Correct: “Freedom of information requests are pleas for government records.”
    - Incorrect: “Freedom of information requests, pleas for government records.”

14. **No invented sensory details or physical actions in nonfiction** – if the source record does not confirm an event occurred, do not report it as fact.
    - Hypothetical risk must be framed explicitly as possibility, not reported fact.
    - Correct: “The badge could open those doors. He still had it.”
    - Incorrect: “He held the badge up to the reader and heard the lock click.” (if unconfirmed)

## Application Notes from Chapter One

- **Experiential writing**: Ground each beat in a physical place and moment. Show Muneeb’s hands on the keyboard, the silence of an empty office, the cursor blinking, the weight of a badge, the sound of backup tapes.
- **Paragraph endings**: Ensure each paragraph finishes with a concrete image or action (e.g., “The dismissal notice blinked on his laptop screen,” “He slipped off his headset,” “The backup tapes whirred as workers tried to restore what was lost”).
- **Technical terms**: Explain through consequence. Instead of leaving “DROP DATABASE dhsprodb” unexplained, add “The command told the database to erase itself.”
- **Definitions via impact**: Define “freedom of information requests” as “pleas for government records” and “equal opportunity files” as “records of hiring, promotions, and discrimination complaints.”
- **Passive voice**: Rewrite passive constructions to active where possible (e.g., “The deletions exposed a flaw” → “The deletions laid bare a flaw”). Keep passive under 10% of clauses.

This style model is the immutable reference for all chapter rewrites and future works in this series.
