# Chapter One: Zero Hour

Muneeb Akhter clicked End on the Teams call at 4:50 p.m.
His headset fell silent.
The dismissal notice blinked on his laptop screen.

His login stayed live six minutes after termination.
The company had not shut his account down.
That active link kept his cursor on the federal login screen.

At 4:56 p.m. he typed his credentials.
He entered a Department of Homeland Security database managed by the contractor.
He moved inside the company’s internal network.

Two minutes later, at 4:58 p.m., he pressed enter after typing: DROP DATABASE dhsprodb
The command told the database to erase itself.
The homeland security production database vanished from the servers.

At 4:59 p.m. Muneeb opened an artificial intelligence chat box.
He asked how to wipe logs from database servers after removing databases.
The question glowed in the chat box.

For forty‑seven minutes the brothers worked side by side.
They deleted databases that ran case management and freedom of information requests.
Freedom of information requests are pleas for government records.
Twelve federal agencies watched their screens go dark.

The brothers copied sensitive data during the spree.
They moved eighteen hundred five equal opportunity files to a flash drive.
Equal opportunity files hold records of hiring, promotions, and discrimination complaints.
They saved four hundred fifty taxpayer records on the same storage device.
Each record held a name, an address, a slice of someone’s life.
Government data spilled onto the flash drive.

Muneeb kept his identification badge after losing his job.
The laminated badge, with his photo and chip, still opened federal doors.
He held the badge up to the reader and heard the lock click.

At 5:52 p.m. the deletion sequence finished.
Ninety‑six United States databases stopped functioning.
The deletions left ninety‑six silent servers.

Court documents confirm the deletions halted investigations.
Agencies delayed payments while they restored data from backups.
Backups covered only some systems.
The backup tapes whirred as workers tried to restore what was lost.

Company policy required immediate account deletion upon termination.
Federal contracts included the same rule.
His account should have flashed 'access denied' within minutes.

Instead his credentials worked for nearly sixty minutes.
That window enabled the destruction.
The brothers watched the deletion progress bar race to completion.

The failure exposed a core weakness.
Contractors ran the operations while the government absorbed the blame.
Departing employees clipped their badges to their belts as they walked out.
